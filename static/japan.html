Accommodation
Kyoto: near Umekōji-Kyōtonishi Station (JR Sagano Line, 1 stop from Kyoto Station). Tokyo: near Inaricho Station (Ginza Line), walking distance to Ueno. Both locations are central with easy access to all sightseeing areas.
Transportation
Get IC cards (Suica or PASMO) for each family member at Haneda. Works on all trains, subways, buses, plus convenience stores and vending machines. No JR Pass needed—you’re buying individual Nozomi tickets for the two shinkansen legs (Shinagawa→Kyoto and Kyoto→Tokyo).
Golden Week Warning
Apr 29 – May 5 is Golden Week, Japan’s biggest holiday period. Your Tokyo days fall right in it. Expect larger crowds at popular attractions, busier trains, and packed airports on May 3–5. Book any reservations (restaurants, museums, Shibuya Sky) well in advance.
Luggage Forwarding
Use Takkyubin (at convenience stores or your accommodation) to send bags ahead. Ship Kyoto→Tokyo bags the evening of Day 6 (Apr 27)—they’ll arrive at your Tokyo accommodation by the time you get there on Day 7. Costs ~¥2,000–3,000 per bag. Traveling light through Nara is much easier.
Convenience Stores
7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart are incredible in Japan. ATMs, meals, drinks, snacks, and Takkyubin drop-off. Perfect for quick breakfasts.
Coin Lockers
Available at every major train station. Kyoto Station has large-size lockers (¥700) for suitcases. Essential on the Nara transfer day.
Cash
Many smaller restaurants, temples, and stalls require cash. Carry ¥10,000–20,000 per day for a family of four.
Etiquette
Remove shoes when entering temples/restaurants/ryokans. Bow slightly. Don’t tip. Be quiet on trains. Don’t eat while walking.
Heian Shrine
Not included due to time, but if you have a free hour near Okazaki (east central Kyoto), it’s worth a visit. Enormous vermillion torii gate and beautiful gardens (¥600).
